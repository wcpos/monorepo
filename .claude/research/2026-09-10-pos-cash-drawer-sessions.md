# Prior-art: how POS systems model the cash drawer session

Research for [wcpos/roadmap#204](https://github.com/wcpos/roadmap/issues/204) (part of #202). Primary sources only — official API references, help centres and source code — read 2026-09-10. Sections 1–2 are facts and citations; §3–§5 are the distillation the ticket asks for. Anything I could not follow to an official URL is marked **unverified**. Revel Systems was not reached (docs gated) and is omitted rather than guessed.

## 2. Per-system notes

### Square

The Cash Drawer Shifts API is explicitly "a reporting API for businesses that use a cash drawer with their Square Point of Sale terminals" — three GETs (`/v2/cash-drawers/shifts`, `/{shift_id}`, `/{shift_id}/events`) and **no create, end or close endpoint**; shifts exist only because someone opened one on the tablet ([guide](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/cashdrawershift-api/reporting), [API index](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/cash-drawers-api)). The webhooks tab says verbatim "This API does not support webhooks in the current version" ([webhooks](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/cash-drawers-api/webhooks)); polling is the only option.

**Keying** is device + location — "for a particular device, in a particular location" — with team members as attributes: `team_member_ids[]` ("The IDs of all team members that were logged into Square Point of Sale"), plus `opening_team_member_id`, `ending_team_member_id`, `closing_team_member_id` ([CashDrawerShift](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/objects/CashDrawerShift)). Multi-cashier on one drawer is the designed case.

**Three states, and the middle one is the interesting one.** `OPEN`; `ENDED` — "ended but has not yet had an employee content audit"; `CLOSED` — "closed with a completed employee content audit and recorded result" ([CashDrawerShiftState](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/enums/CashDrawerShiftState)). The help centre puts it plainly: "Ending a cash drawer is different than closing a cash drawer" ([8344](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8344-start-and-end-a-cash-drawer-session)). Ending freezes the expected number; closing records what a human found. Forgotten shifts auto-end at 30 days open with 7 days of inactivity — to `ENDED`, not `CLOSED`. Reopening: no documented mechanism (**unverified**).

**Events** are the ledger: `NO_SALE`, `CASH_TENDER_PAYMENT`, `OTHER_TENDER_PAYMENT`, `CASH_TENDER_CANCELLED_PAYMENT`, `OTHER_TENDER_CANCELLED_PAYMENT`, `CASH_TENDER_REFUND`, `OTHER_TENDER_REFUND`, `PAID_IN`, `PAID_OUT` ([enum](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/enums/CashDrawerEventType)). Non-cash tenders appear as **zero-amount** events — the drawer opening is recorded, the money is not.

**The count**: `opened_cash_money`, `cash_payment_money`, `cash_refunds_money`, `cash_paid_in_money`, `cash_paid_out_money`, `expected_cash_money`, `closed_cash_money` — the middle four "computed by summing their respective event types". `expected_cash_money` "can be negative if employees have not correctly recorded all the events". **There is no variance field** on either `CashDrawerShift` or `CashDrawerShiftSummary`. The summary object is deliberately thin — opened, expected, closed and nothing else — so `ListCashDrawerShifts` returns summaries and any breakdown costs a second call ([CashDrawerShiftSummary](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/objects/CashDrawerShiftSummary)).

**No sale→shift link exists in either direction.** `Tender` has no `cash_drawer_shift_id`; `Payment` has none; `CashDrawerShiftEvent` carries no payment, order or tender id — only `id`, `event_type`, `event_money`, `created_at`, `description`, `team_member_id` ([CashDrawerShiftEvent](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/objects/CashDrawerShiftEvent), [Tender](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/objects/Tender)).

Cash management is enabled per device or per mode, with a default starting cash and report email preferences ([5152](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5152-cash-drawer-management)). In Square for Restaurants, "Totaling cash drawers" is one of four togglable Close of Day prerequisites, and "Overriding any close of day procedures requires the owner passcode" ([6594](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6594-end-of-day-reporting-with-square-for-restaurants)). Blind count and offline drawer behaviour: not documented (**unverified**).

### Shopify POS

Two generations coexist. `CashTrackingSession` "Tracks the balance in a cash drawer for a point of sale device over the course of a shift" ([object](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/CashTrackingSession)); from API 2026-04, `PointOfSaleDevicePaymentSession` "replaces `CashTrackingSession` as the main cash session resource" and a new `CashDrawer` resource "decouples cash management from individual POS devices… Multiple devices can connect to a single drawer" ([changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/new-retail-cash-management-capabilities), [CashDrawer](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/CashDrawer)). Before that, "Only one session can be open per device at a time" ([POS sessions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/cash-register-management/register-sessions-in-shopify-pos)). Sessions record `openingStaffMember`/`closingStaffMember`; each movement carries its own `staffMember`.

**Shopify is the only vendor with a real write API for this**: `pointOfSaleDevicePaymentSessionOpen/Close/Adjust/Count`, each taking `staffMemberId`, optional `note`, `reasonCodeId` and `time`; close "will calculate expected balance and discrepancies". Plus `cashDrawerCreate`, `cashDrawerFindOrCreate`, `pointOfSaleDeviceAssignToCashDrawer` ([object](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/PointOfSaleDevicePaymentSession)).

**The float is a pair, and that is the design idea worth stealing**: `expectedOpeningBalance` = "The amount expected to be in the cash drawer based on the previous session" vs `openingBalance` = "The counted cash balance when the session was opened". Admin reporting then splits variance into **Opening discrepancy** and **Closing discrepancy** ([reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/cash-register-management/cash-tracking-reports-by-location)). An unopened session auto-opens on the first cash payment "using the prior session's calculated closing balance as the new float. For a store's very first session, this begins at 0". At close you count, then "enter the desired float and remove excess cash so only the float remains" — the close writes the next open.

**Auto-transitions are first-class reason codes**, not silent: `AUTO_END_SESSION_LOCATION_CHANGE`, `AUTO_END_SESSION_LOGOUT`, `AUTO_START_SESSION_CHECKOUT`, alongside `CASH_COUNT`, `CASH_PAYOUT`, `CASH_PICKUP`, `CHANGE_CORRECTION`, `OTHER`, `PETTY_CASH`, `TIP_PAYOUT` ([enum](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/enums/CashManagementDefaultReasonCodeEnum)). Reason codes are opt-in per merchant, and custom codes can be added ([union](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/unions/CashManagementReasonCode)).

"Closed sessions cannot be reopened or edited." Forgotten sessions are handled by disclaimer, not by rule: "Register sessions aren't for long term reporting. If you leave a register session open for too long, then the Expected cash and Discrepancies reports might become inaccurate." The denomination counting tool exists but "supports only the following currencies: AUD, CAD, EUR, GBP, JPY, NZD, PLN, SGD, and USD". It is explicitly **not blind** — even with *Manage payment tracking* denied, "cash expected will still be visible" ([POS permissions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/permissions/pos-permissions)).

Sale linkage is real: `CashTrackingSession.cashTransactions: OrderTransactionConnection!`, and `OrderTransaction.device`/`.user`/`.location`. There is **no webhook topic** for cash or sessions; real-time events exist only inside POS UI extensions (**unverified** — target pages 404'd on direct fetch). The feature is POS Pro-gated, and one device setting governs whether it does anything: **Require cash counting at session start and end** — "If this is deactivated, then sessions still track payment data, but won't explicitly track cash amounts."

### Lightspeed X-Series (Vend lineage)

Register-keyed, hard: "A register can only be used by one device at a time… you must create a register for each device" ([outlets and registers](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25534178722459-Setting-up-outlets-and-registers)). The closure object carries **no user field at all** — its `register` sub-object is `{id, name, outlet_id, outlet_name}` ([webhook payloads](https://x-series-api.lightspeedhq.com/docs/webhooks_example_payloads)). Multi-cashier attribution is per-sale via the `ask_for_user_on_sale` register setting.

**Opening is implicit and the clocks disagree.** "A register is opened when you first navigate to the Retail POS Sell screen. Retail POS checks the date and time on your computer or iPad, recording it as your register's open time" — while the close "connects to the Retail POS cloud server and checks its date and time" ([closure report](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25534252854043-Using-the-register-closure-report)). Registers also **auto-open** when a Xero sync or an eCommerce order posts a payment after close ([why](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25533799810971-Why-did-my-Register-open-automatically)).

Everything after the close is frozen: "once the register has been closed it isn't possible to edit the register… you can enter more/less than you have counted to offset the balance in your next closure" ([reopen?](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25534022428315-Can-I-reopen-or-edit-a-register-closure)); the float can't be edited either. All roles can open, close and move cash — "These permissions are the default and cannot be removed from user roles". Enabling Cash Management is one-way: "Once it has been enabled, this feature cannot be disabled."

Four movement types, all with notes: **Cash out** and **Cash in** ("goes directly to/from the bank") and **Petty cash out**/**Petty cash in** ("does not go directly to/from the bank"), plus report-only **Opening Float**, **Closing Float** and **Float Adjustment** = "discrepancies between the opening and closing float" ([meanings](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25534268781851-What-does-each-cash-movement-mean-from-the-register-closure-report)). Expected is per payment type and includes the float; counting is by total or by denomination with a live **Difference**; variance is named **shortfall** / **overpayment**. It is not blind — the close screen doubles as a documented spot-check tool you can back out of. At close "Retail POS will assume that nothing is left in your cash drawer".

**The API is a stub.** `PUT /registers/{id}/actions/open` takes only `register_open_time` — **no float field**; `PUT .../actions/close` takes `payments[]` of `{payment_type_id, total}` — counted totals only, no denominations, no float, no variance ([open](https://x-series-api.lightspeedhq.com/reference/openregister), [close](https://x-series-api.lightspeedhq.com/reference/closeregister)). There is no `register_closures` GET; the closure exists only as a `register_closure.create` webhook payload, which is far richer: `payments_summary{…, discrepancy}` and `payment_counts[]` of `{start_total, payments_total, withdrawals_total, close_total}` — the drawer arithmetic, exposed once, one-way. `GET /registers/{id}/payments_summary` "should be presented to the user as the 'expected' state of the register" ([closing](https://x-series-api.lightspeedhq.com/docs/registers_closing)).

Sales link three ways, all id-based: `Sale.source.register_id`, `Sale.metadata.complete_open_sequence_id` ("The sequence ID of the register open at the time the sale was completed"), and the same on payments and line items ([sale](https://x-series-api.lightspeedhq.com/reference/getsalebyid)). And yet offline still breaks it: offline sales synced later are a documented cause of "sales appear in the wrong register closure", and such a sale **cannot be moved** ([why](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/25534011570971-Why-do-sales-appear-in-the-wrong-register-closure)). Closing itself is online-only.

### Lightspeed R-Series

Cleaner data model, older product. `RegisterCount` is "the end of day (or shift) count of the cash in a specific Register", with `openTime`, `createTime` ("the date/time the register was closed"), `openEmployeeID`, `closeEmployeeID` and child `RegisterCountAmounts` — each one the pair `calculated` ("The amount the system calculated from payments that the register should have of this payment type") vs `actual` ("The physical amount counted") ([RegisterCount](https://developers.lightspeedhq.com/retail/endpoints/RegisterCount/), [RegisterCountAmount](https://developers.lightspeedhq.com/retail/endpoints/RegisterCountAmount/)).

Two ideas here are unique. **The float is not a field** — `RegisterWithdraw` is one signed object for every cash movement, and "The opening count for a register is a positive RegisterWithdraw" ([ref](https://developers.lightspeedhq.com/retail/endpoints/RegisterWithdraw/)). And expected is **exposed as its constituents**: `GET /Register/{id}/calculated.json` returns `{add, withdraw, payment, paymentTypeID}` per payment type ([ref](https://developers.lightspeedhq.com/retail/endpoints/Register-calculated/)), so a client can show the derivation rather than a number. Counts remain editable after submission, which X-Series forbids — same vendor, opposite rulings ([R-Series open/close](https://retail-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260800790810-Opening-and-closing-a-register)).

### Clover

**Clover does not model a cash drawer session.** No till, drawer-session or closeout object exists in the REST reference. What exists is an append-only log, readable three ways — all merchant, by employee, by device — with `filter` fields `[employee.id, note, amountChange, employee.name, type, device.id, timestamp]` ([cash_events](https://docs.clover.com/dev/reference/cashgetallcashevents)). No POST, PUT or DELETE.

The `type` enum is exactly six values: **`LOAD`, `TRANSACTION`, `OPEN`, `ADJUSTMENT`, `COUNT`, `UNLOAD`** ([SDK Type](https://clover.github.io/clover-android-sdk/clover-android-sdk/com.clover.sdk.v3.cash/-type/index.html)). `CashEvent` is `{type, amountChange, timestamp, note, employee, device, merchant}` — no order id, no payment id. A `COUNT` therefore carries an amount and a free-text note and nothing else: no expected, no variance, no denominations. The device SDK exposes exactly `addCash(long amount, String note)` and `removeCash(long amount, String note)`, and states the contract's purpose bluntly — it "should not be used not for cash payments or refunds" ([CashEvents](https://clover.github.io/clover-android-sdk/clover-android-sdk/com.clover.sdk.v3.cash/-cash-events/index.html)).

**`Shift` is a time clock, not a drawer.** `POST /v3/merchants/{mId}/employees/{empId}/shifts` takes `employee`, `cashTipsCollected`, `serverBanking`, `inTime`, `outTime` and the three override fields — **no device, no cash amount other than tips** ([ref](https://docs.clover.com/dev/reference/employeecreateshift)). The one cash-adjacent hook is `serverBanking` — the employee ticks "I'm holding my cash sales during this shift" at clock-in. Webhook key `CA` fires when a "Cash log event occurs"; there is no shift webhook (`SH` is *service hour*, i.e. business hours) ([webhooks](https://docs.clover.com/dev/docs/webhooks)). The merchant `properties` object's `manualCloseout`/`autoCloseoutTimezone` fields are **card batch** closeout, not drawer closeout ([properties](https://docs.clover.com/dev/reference/merchantgetmerchantproperties)).

### Loyverse

The tightest single-object model of the lot, and read-only. `Shift` is keyed `store_id` + `pos_device_id`, with `opened_by_employee` / `closed_by_employee` as attributes, and carries in one payload: `starting_cash`, `cash_payments`, `cash_refunds`, `paid_in`, `paid_out`, `expected_cash`, `actual_cash`, plus `gross_sales`, `refunds`, `discounts`, `net_sales`, `tip`, `surcharge`, `taxes[]`, `payments[]` by type, and an embedded `cash_movements[]` of `{type, money_amount, comment, employee_id, created_at}` where `type` is `PAY_IN | PAY_OUT` and "The value is always positive" ([API spec](https://developer.loyverse.com/docs/API-Reference__v1.0.yaml)). So the shift is device-keyed while each movement inside it is employee-attributed — exactly the shape multi-cashier needs.

There is **no status field** (open ⇔ `closed_at` absent) and **no difference field** (derive `actual_cash − expected_cash`). Scope is `SHIFTS_READ`; there is no `SHIFTS_WRITE` and no POST. Receipts carry `pos_device_id` but **no `shift_id`**, and `/receipts` cannot be filtered by shift — linkage is a device + time-window join.

Two behaviours worth copying. **Blind count is a permission, not a mode**: disable *View shift report* and "cashiers will only see the Actual cash amount field when closing the shift" ([shift management](https://help.loyverse.com/help/shift-management-loyverse-pos)). And **shifts work offline** — "make sales and manage shifts even without an Internet connection"; shift opening, closing, pay-ins and payouts are all syncable events and show as *Unsynced* in POS shift history until they land ([offline](https://help.loyverse.com/help/offline-work-of-pos), [shift history](https://help.loyverse.com/help/how-work-shift-history-pos)). The webhook `shifts.create` is described as firing "when a **closed** shift is created" — i.e. on close, not on open. The feature is off by default (Settings → Features → Shifts).

### Odoo POS (source read on branch `18.0`)

`pos.session` is keyed to `config_id` (the register), with `user_id` = "Opened By"; a constraint blocks a second live session per config — "Another session is already opened for this point of sale" — with `rescue` sessions exempt ([pos_session.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/18.0/addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py)). Several cashiers share one session under `module_pos_hr`, switching by PIN or badge; only *advanced rights* employees may "Close the current POS session" ([employee login](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/point_of_sale/employee_login.html)).

**Four states, gating both ends**: `('opening_control', 'Opening Control')`, `('opened', 'In Progress')`, `('closing_control', 'Closing Control')`, `('closed', 'Closed & Posted')`. `action_pos_session_open` **seeds the float from the last close** — `cash_register_balance_start = last_session.cash_register_balance_end_real` — and `set_opening_control(cashbox_value, notes)` computes `difference = cashbox_value - cash_register_balance_start` and posts an "Opening cash" chatter message before overwriting the start. That is an opening variance, recorded as narrative rather than as a field.

Expected is `cash_register_balance_end` ("Theoretical Closing Balance", help: "Opening balance summed to all cash transactions") = start + statement lines + cash payments; counted is `cash_register_balance_end_real` ("Ending Balance"); `cash_register_difference` ("Before Closing Difference") = real − theoretical. Uniquely, **the difference is posted to the ledger**: `_post_statement_difference` writes a statement line to the cash journal's `loss_account_id` or `profit_account_id`, and refuses to close if those accounts are missing.

**What blocks a close**: draft orders — "You cannot close the POS when orders are still in draft" — and unposted invoices. A threshold gate exists on `pos.config`: `set_maximum_difference` ("Set a maximum difference allowed between the expected and counted money during the closing of the session") with `amount_authorized_diff`, above which a non-manager "will have an error message at the closing of his session saying that he needs to contact his manager" ([pos_config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/18.0/addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_config.py)). If the accounting move won't balance, the transaction is rolled back and a **"Force Close Session"** wizard posts a balancing line named 'Difference at closing PoS session'. `_alert_old_session` raises an activity on a session open for 7 days.

**Rescue sessions** are the forgotten-session answer: "Auto-generated session for orphan orders, ignored in constraints", exempt from the uniqueness constraint, auto-computing their own closing cash, and carrying a source comment that "It is not yet possible to close a rescue session through the front end". Cash in/out is a menu action taking amount + reason and writing an `account.bank.statement.line`; the counting calculator "computes the total amount in the cash drawer depending on the coins and bills counted", writes the total into the **Counted** column and the breakdown into **Notes** ([docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/point_of_sale.html)). Sales carry `session_id` directly.

### Toast

Toast models the **drawer**, not a session, and the drawer's life is the session. States: **Open**, **Paused** ("no longer accepting entries, but cash hasn't been counted" — shown as "TO BE COUNTED", and pausing spawns a fresh drawer inheriting the name plus a suffix and the same starting balance, e.g. "Bar" → "Bar (2)"), and **Closed** ([cash drawers](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawers.html)). Fields are `Starting balance`, `Expected balance` ("starting balance plus the total of all cash entries"), the counted actual, and `Cash overage/shortage`. Drawers auto-close at the 4 a.m. closeout; **Reset Balance Type** is Automatic ("a new one opens with your defined starting balance") or Manual ([setup](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Setting-Up-Cash-Drawers)). Closed drawers can be reopened via **Adjust closing entries**.

Employee binding is optional and named: **Cash in Hand** ("uses a personal cash bank… not assigned to a cash drawer") vs **Cash in Drawer**, plus a per-shift **Lock drawer to me**. **Blind count is a permission pair** — `Manager > 3.18 Cash Drawers (Full)` sees expected balances; `Manager > 3.17 Cash Drawers (Blind)` does not. A **Cash drawer variance** page sets **Closeout Over/Short Max**, above which "managerial override is required to close the cash drawer" ([cash drawer operations](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerOperations.html) — read via the official docs' search index; direct fetch returned 403).

The API is read-only and business-date scoped: `GET /cashmgmt/v1/entries?businessDate=yyyyMMdd` and `GET /cashmgmt/v1/deposits` ([guide](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiUsingCashManagementApi.html), [deposits](https://doc.toasttab.com/openapi/cashmanagement/operation/depositsGet/)). Entry types: `CASH_IN`, `CASH_COLLECTED`, `CASH_OUT`, `NO_SALE`, `PAY_OUT`, `TIP_OUT`, `UNDO_PAY_OUT`, `DRIVER_REIMBURSEMENT`, `CLOSE_OUT_EXACT`, `CLOSE_OUT_OVERAGE`, `CLOSE_OUT_SHORTAGE`. `CashEntry` is `{guid, date, reason, amount (signed), payoutReason, cashDrawer, undoes, noSaleReason, employee1, employee2, type}` — `employee1` acted, `employee2` approved, `undoes` points at the entry being reversed. **Nothing is deleted**: undoing a `PAY_OUT` writes an `UNDO_PAY_OUT` with reason "Undo Pay Out"; undoing a `CASH_IN` writes a `CASH_OUT`. Note the close result is encoded **as three entry types**, so the sign is a type and the number must be recomputed.

### The tail

**Erply** — `POSOpenDay` takes `pointOfSaleID`, `openedSum` ("Total amount of cash in register at opening time"), `openedUnixTime` and an optional `employeeID` (defaulting to the API caller), returning a `dayID`; `POSCloseDay` takes `closedSum` ("Total amount of money left in register") **and** `bankedSum` ("Total amount of money removed from register and deposited") ([POSOpenDay](https://learn-api.erply.com/requests/posopenday), [POSCloseDay](https://learn-api.erply.com/requests/poscloseday)). It is the only system that splits the closing count by destination, and the only one with **per-tender variance reasons**: `reasonID`, `counted-CHECK`/`counted-CARD-VISA`, `varianceReasonID-*`. Both calls are deprecated in favour of `POSOpenDayV2` / `POST /v1/shift/close`, V2 explicitly to support "counting drawers (cashiers carrying a drawer between checkout stations)".

**SumUp POS** has a real till session: enter the "initial cash fund" at open (with a "Default cash fund" mode that suppresses the prompt), close via Settings → Till management → "Close the Till / Z Ticket"; an open order "will not allow you to close the till" ([open/close](https://help.sumup.com/en-GB/pos/articles/75000023446-open-close-how-to-open-close-your-till-and-fill-in-your-cash-fund-)).

**Zettle (PayPal Point of Sale)** has **no cash drawer session**. The developer portal documents Purchase, Finance, Inventory and Product Library only ([API overview](https://developer.zettle.com/docs/api)); the drawer pops on a cash payment or receipt print via a connected printer, and cash reaches reporting as a payment-method breakdown.

## 3. Best of everything

### Shared conventions — adopt without further debate

1. **The session is keyed to the register, never to the cashier.** Square (device + location), Shopify (device, now drawer), Lightspeed (register), Loyverse (`store_id` + `pos_device_id`), Odoo (`config_id`), Erply (`pointOfSaleID`), Toast (hardware drawer). Not one vendor keys it to a person. The cashier appears as *opened by*, *closed by* and a per-row actor.
2. **Several cashiers on one open drawer is the normal case**, solved by stamping the actor on each row: Square's plural `team_member_ids[]`, Loyverse's `cash_movements[].employee_id`, Odoo's PIN switch, Lightspeed's `ask_for_user_on_sale`.
3. **Five quantities, one formula.** opening float + cash sales − cash refunds + paid in − paid out = expected. Square's seven money fields, Loyverse's seven, Lightspeed's `payment_counts{start_total, payments_total, withdrawals_total, close_total}` and Odoo's `_compute_cash_balance` are the same arithmetic in four dialects.
4. **Paid in / paid out are their own rows, positive, with direction in the type** (Square: "must not be negative"; Loyverse: "The value is always positive"). Signed amounts are the minority (R-Series, Toast) and read worse.
5. **Every movement carries an actor and a note.** Universal. Configured *reason codes* on top of the note are the majority (Shopify, Toast, Erply, Odoo); Clover and Square have only free text and are the weaker for it.
6. **A no-sale drawer open is a recorded, zero-amount event with its own permission**: Square `NO_SALE`, Toast `NO_SALE` + `noSaleReason`, Clover `OPEN`, Shopify *Open drawer*, Loyverse "Open cash drawer without making a sale".
7. **Nobody stores denominations.** Every denomination UI is a calculator that fills a single counted total; Odoo puts the breakdown in a Notes field. Copy that exactly.
8. **The close is immutable and corrections are new rows** — Lightspeed's "offset the balance in your next closure", Toast's `undoes` inverse entry. (R-Series and Toast's *Adjust closing entries* are the exceptions and both are correction affordances bolted on later.)

### Divergences — pick, with a reason

- **Two-phase close.** Square is alone in `ENDED` vs `CLOSED` ("ended but has not yet had an employee content audit"), but Toast's `Paused`/"TO BE COUNTED" and Odoo's `closing_control` are the same idea. **Take it**: `open → counting → closed`, where entering `counting` freezes expected and stops the register accepting cash. It is the only shape that survives "the manager counts at 9pm what the cashier stopped at 5pm", and Toast shows the pattern that keeps the till selling: pausing spawns a fresh drawer.
- **Float carry-forward.** Odoo seeds from the last counted close; Shopify carries it *and* keeps both numbers (`expectedOpeningBalance` vs `openingBalance`); Lightspeed assumes the drawer is emptied and makes you retype; Square offers a configured constant. **Take Shopify's pair.** Storing both suggested and counted opening is the only way to get an *opening* variance, which is how overnight shrinkage is caught — and it costs one extra column.
- **Blind count.** Toast (`3.17 Blind` vs `3.18 Full`) and Loyverse (revoke *View shift report*) both implement it as a **permission**; Shopify explicitly refuses ("cash expected will still be visible"). Two vendors converged on permission-not-mode; follow them.
- **What blocks a close.** Odoo blocks hard on draft orders; SumUp blocks on open orders; Square Restaurants makes prerequisites configurable with an owner-passcode override; Lightspeed blocks on essentially nothing. **Block on unpaid/parked orders for that register, allow a manager override, and never block on the variance itself** — cap it instead.
- **Variance threshold.** Odoo's `set_maximum_difference`/`amount_authorized_diff` and Toast's `Closeout Over/Short Max` are the same feature invented twice: above the threshold, a manager must approve the close. Adopt it; it is the cheapest fraud control in the set.
- **Reopen vs correct.** Shopify: "Closed sessions cannot be reopened or edited". Lightspeed X: irreversible. Toast and R-Series allow edits. **Choose immutable**, with a correcting adjustment row — roadmap#197's fiscal motivation makes an append-only trail non-negotiable, and Toast's `undoes` field shows how to make a correction legible without a delete.
- **Sale → session linkage: id, not time window.** Odoo stamps `session_id` on the order; Lightspeed stamps `complete_open_sequence_id` on the sale; Shopify hangs `cashTransactions` off the session. Square and Loyverse have **no link at all** and force a device + timestamp join. **Stamp the id at completion.** Lightspeed's own help centre documents the failure mode of the alternative: offline sales synced later "appear in the wrong register closure" and cannot be moved.
- **Which tenders get counted.** Lightspeed and Erply count *every* payment type at close; Square, Loyverse, Shopify and Odoo count cash and report the rest. **Count cash; report the rest.** Counting card totals is a card-batch job, not a drawer job — Clover keeps them separate for exactly this reason.
- **Expected as a number vs as its parts.** R-Series' `/calculated` returns `{add, withdraw, payment}`; Lightspeed's closure payload returns `start_total/payments_total/withdrawals_total/close_total`. Return the parts, not just the total — a cashier who is $5 short needs to see which line moved.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. **Don't ship a report-only session.** Square, Loyverse and Clover all expose cash drawer data read-only, so no third-party client can open or close a drawer through their APIs. WCPOS *is* the client; the write path is the product.
2. **Don't infer session membership from a time window** (Square, Loyverse, Clover). It breaks the first time a sale syncs late — which for an offline-first POS is the normal case, not the edge case.
3. **Don't auto-open silently.** Lightspeed opens a register merely because someone navigated to the Sell screen, and again when a Xero sync posts a payment after close; Shopify auto-opens on the first cash payment. Shopify at least records *why*, as `AUTO_START_SESSION_CHECKOUT`. If a session can open without a human, the reason must be a stored code, or "opened by" becomes a lie.
4. **Don't take the open time from the device clock and the close time from the server clock** — Lightspeed does exactly this, which makes the session's own duration unverifiable.
5. **Don't encode the variance as a type.** Toast's `CLOSE_OUT_EXACT` / `_OVERAGE` / `_SHORTAGE` gives you the sign but not the number.
6. **Don't ship a thin list object.** Square's `CashDrawerShiftSummary` carries opened/expected/closed and nothing else, so every list view needs N+1 detail calls.
7. **Don't overload "shift".** Clover's `Shift` is a time clock with no device field and no cash but tips; Toast's Shift Review is labour. Reserve the word.
8. **Don't make enabling it irreversible** — Lightspeed X: "Once it has been enabled, this feature cannot be disabled."
9. **Don't answer the forgotten session with a disclaimer.** Shopify's is "reports might become inaccurate". Square auto-ends at 30 days open + 7 idle; Odoo raises an activity at 7 days and invents *rescue sessions* for orphan orders. Pick a rule and act on it.
10. **Don't leave the physical assumption unstated.** Lightspeed assumes the drawer is emptied at close; Toast reopens with the starting balance; Shopify asks you to leave the float in and take the rest. Same number, three different meanings — say which one your close means, in the UI.
11. **Don't allow deletes.** Toast's undo is an inverse entry pointing at what it reverses.

## 4. Proposed canonical vocabulary

| Term | Means | Rejected alternatives, and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Register** | The till identity (roadmap#197), stamped on orders | *Device* — a device is hardware and can be reinstalled; *terminal* — reads as card terminal |
| **Cash drawer session** (short: **session**) | The bounded open→close period of cash accountability for one register | *Shift* — labour/time clock everywhere (Clover, Toast); *drawer* — hardware; *day* — Erply's, wrong the moment a session crosses midnight, which Lightspeed explicitly allows; *closure* — names the object after its ending |
| **Opening float** | Cash counted into the drawer at open | *Starting cash*, *cash fund*, *opening balance* — all in use, none clearer; keep *float* as the noun and pair it with **expected opening float** carried from the last close |
| **Cash movement** | A `paid_in` or `paid_out` row: positive amount, direction in the type, plus actor, reason code and note | *Adjustment* (Clover/Shopify) — sounds like a correction; *withdraw* (R-Series) — one-directional name for a two-directional thing |
| **No sale** | A zero-amount drawer open, with a reason | — (universal) |
| **Expected cash** | opening float + cash sales − cash refunds + paid in − paid out | *Theoretical* (Odoo), *calculated* (R-Series) — accountant's words; merchants read *expected* |
| **Counted cash** | The number a human entered at close | *Actual* (Loyverse, R-Series) — presumes the count is true; the whole point of a variance is that it may not be |
| **Variance** | counted − expected. Positive = **over**, negative = **short** | *Discrepancy* (Shopify) — presumes error; *difference* (Odoo) — used there for the pre-close preview |
| **Cashier** | The person; recorded as `opened_by`, `closed_by`, and `actor` on every row | *Employee*, *team member*, *user* — all vendor-local |
| States | `open` → `counting` → `closed` | Square's `ENDED` is the right *state*, the wrong *word* — it says nothing about what is pending |

## 5. Relating to WCPOS

WCPOS has no session model at all today: orders carry `_pos_user` and `_pos_store`, `next`'s payment ledger rows carry `cashier_id`, and register identity is only now arriving via [roadmap#197](https://github.com/wcpos/roadmap/issues/197). That ordering is fortunate rather than late — every system above keys the session to the register, so #197's `register` entity is the prerequisite, and the session should be keyed to it and carry `opened_by`/`closed_by` cashier ids as attributes, not as the key. Two WCPOS-specific facts should force choices the prior art leaves open. First, offline sales ride a sync queue, so the session id must be **stamped on the order at completion on the device** and travel with it — Lightspeed proves that a server-side time-window join puts late-syncing sales in the wrong closure permanently, and Loyverse proves that a device can open and close a session offline if the session's own open/close/pay-in/pay-out are queued events like any other. Second, WCPOS is the *client*, not the reporting API: the three vendors with the richest session objects (Square, Loyverse, Clover) all expose them read-only, which is precisely the gap a WooCommerce-native POS can fill — the session should be a first-class WooCommerce-side resource with a real write path, an immutable close, and a correcting-adjustment row instead of an edit.
